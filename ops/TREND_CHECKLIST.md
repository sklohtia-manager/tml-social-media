## For each candidate topic or breakout example, record
`link`, `region` (AU / IN / Hindi-speaking audience), `publicationDate`, `observedMetric` (the exact figure and what it measures, e.g. "views", "search interest 0–100"), `dateChecked`, and a **class**:
- **verified-breakout:** a post, video or query seen to be performing well above its own channel's or topic's norm, with the link and metric recorded;
- **news-interest:** ordinary interest around a dated release or event (for example an ABS or RBI release);
- **evergreen:** steady, season-free demand (for example "what is net worth").
A topic isn't "trending" unless it is `verified-breakout`. Never label from memory or guess a metric.

## If a source can't be checked
Write exactly what couldn't be checked and why, for example "Google Trends: unreachable from the cloud session (no response)". Never fill the gap with estimates. Private analytics (Studio, native insights) are "unavailable" until the owner exports or reads them out.

## Then
1. Compare candidates with `content/history.json` (no repeat; the 21-day rule for educational subjects) and the calendar (`ops/EDITORIAL_CALENDAR.md`).
2. Verify every figure on a primary source before drafting (RUNBOOK §2).
3. Tag the subject and class in the calendar. A trend never overrides the compliance gate: no advice, trading, speculation or fast-money framing, even when it's popular.
